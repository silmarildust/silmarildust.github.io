I’m sorry for hiding that picture of us folded in my notebook because I just couldn’t look at your face without having a crisis
I’m sorry for the attachment I have to the broken dollar store earphones that were shoved, tangled, in the deep pockets of my messenger bag the pair that we shared on that bench in the middle of the field listening to The Wallows after walking for three hours, our hands intertwined the whole time and I didn’t know how to tell you that my fingers were growing numb I’m sorry for the way I look at the stuffed owl with the floppy beak that I bring around and the stuffed bear with no clothes that I have worn down over the nights yes, the one you called “dead meat” because it reminded you of a corpse the same way I say I look at you and your brimming eyes, so full of adoration for me? or for what I say I am? I’m sorry that I can’t sit beside you and give you everything you give me and that I can’t bring myself to say the words “don’t be afraid, I’ll always be your safe space” I’m sorry that I looked you in the eyes that January night and said everything I once meant and for how I took your hands into mine and ignored the burning in my chest because I have woven so many mistruths about misfortunes that I no longer know if you love me for me I’m sorry I hid the picture we took at the arcade booth where we made a heart with our hands for so long squeezed between ink-stained pages where I wrote about you after you gave your heart to me, after I became your world and you became my greatest fear you can’t just open your mouth and whisper words of devotion–don’t you dare call me perfect everything I’ve ever been afraid of has permanent creases on it This poem won me the title of Commended Poet (in the top 100 out of over 15,000 participants internationally) during The Foyle Young Poets of the Year Award in 2023.

what have you escaped from?
the weight of voices crashing against the walls sharp — so sudden, like thunder that never faded they settled into the spaces i tried to make my own heavy — no, constant, pressing down until silence felt safer than being seen the eyes that lingered too long, quiet but heavy tracing shadows over my skin they measured me against things i never chose shrinking me with every glance that held more questions than answers the emptiness that stretched wide filling rooms with a quiet hum it slipped and slithered into corners waiting for me to notice how much space i left untouched the race i never asked to run every step slipped beneath me every breath pushed me faster second place was failure standing still felt like falling the mirror that stared back, never wavering asking questions i couldn’t answer its surface rippled with doubt each reflection unfamiliar like it knew something i didn’t and the thoughts that once floated light and unbothered skimming the surface of things too vast to hold too distant to touch but there is a place where the weight drifts away where the silence softens and the currents run deep where the surface breaks open and something quiet the endless ocean finally pulls me under
just this once (only the current knows)
I write like the tide, slipping between rocks and salt, leaving only traces of foam that vanish before they’re seen. The currents know where I’ve been, but they never speak. My words sink quietly, silt settling over meaning until nothing is clear, only the soft weight of water pressing against the seabed, where roots twist in silence and the light forgets to reach. You search for a path, but the ground shifts beneath you, sand sliding into deeper darkness. There is no map, only whispers of things long drowned, their voices curling through the undertow, half-heard, half-lost. But when I speak to you, it is not the ocean. It is the sky before the storm, heavy like the dawn, a wind that cuts the horizon clean. No riddles. No quiet. Only thunder, only salt, only bone. Still, when I turn back to the water, I slip beneath the surface, leaving no ripples, only silence. And what the sea holds it holds alone.

oscillations in amber
A tremor hums beneath the lacquer, slow threads of motion, or perhaps memory soft collisions of silence folding into deeper shades. Light refracts on unseen boundaries, each glimmer a cipher, each shadow an elegy. Beneath, something shifts, or lingers an impulse, half-formed, pressing against the threadbare hush. The air hums, weightless yet taut, pulling the horizon inward. Time hesitates, coiled in the marrow of stillness, its pulse almost imperceptible.
this too shall pass
with every passing turn of the Earth, a girl is growing out of her skin as her bones threaten to breach flesh. with every strike of the clock at 8:32, her neurons light up with want for a magnet that only tugs at her — it must be broken. with every note of the song bouncing about in her mind, she writes another line that would be etched on her heart, but never any further. with every irking buzz of the phone, a Man utters breathe, yet makes it impossible to do anything but bleed — and this she does. with every glance at the world in her closet, there is excessive and uncomfortable squirming, picking, writhing, and defeat. with every step in the light nighttime rain, intricate tales weaved by years of living in the past resurface — dreaming is the illness of the privileged. with every helpless exchange, she lets go of the damp grassroots crumpled by rubber soles that didn't give a damn. and with every minute spent with only the thick air, the sickness in her stomach dissipates and the wrinkles in the corner of her eyes deepen — teeth crack her face once again.
